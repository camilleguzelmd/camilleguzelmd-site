Care

Swipe sideways for pelvic floor, hypermobility, and neurorehab.

Pelvic floor

Pain. Leaking. Tightness. Pressure. Sex that hurts. The long tail of birth or surgery. I look at muscle, nerve, and joint — the pelvic floor and the rest of the body it belongs to.

Jaw pain and TMJ too. Holding tension upstairs works a lot like the pelvic floor.

Hypermobility

A body that moves farther than it should can still be strong. Pain, instability, fatigue, a scan that looks “normal.” We sort what to load, what to protect, and how you live in it.

For patient education, the Ehlers-Danlos Society is a good place to look — for example ehlers-danlos.com.

Neurorehabilitation and limb difference

After stroke, brain injury, spinal cord injury, cerebral palsy, or another neurologic change, the work is function. Walking. A usable hand. Speech. A day that fits you.

Amputees and limb difference too. Adaptive sport is a particular love — para-athletes who compete hard in bodies others misjudge.

Procedures

Swipe sideways for procedure details.

Botulinum toxin

For spasticity and dystonia (for example neck, trunk, and limbs), using ultrasound and EMG guidance to help target treatment accurately. Therapeutic pelvic-floor and jaw/TMJ uses may be appropriate in some cases; those uses are off-label and decided after individual evaluation.

Trigger point injections

Ultrasound- and EMG-guided. Steroidal and non-steroidal options.

Intra-articular joint injections

Ultrasound-guided — shoulder, knee, hip, among others. Steroidal and non-steroidal options.

About

Dr. Guzel is a physiatrist: a board-certified Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation physician (PM&R MD).

Fellow of the American Academy of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation (FAAPMR).

I take care of adults — pelvic floor, hypermobility, and neurorehab, including limb difference. Spasticity and dystonia. Cerebral palsy as people move into adult care and as they age. Stroke, spinal cord injury, brain injury. When holding tension is the problem — jaw, scapular, pelvic floor. Performing arts medicine is part of how I practice. So is adaptive sport: para-athletes competing hard in bodies the world often underestimates.

For botulinum toxin, I use ultrasound and EMG guidance when treating spasticity and dystonia to help target treatment accurately. Trigger point injections are separate. So are ultrasound-guided joint injections. Some uses — including certain pelvic-floor and jaw treatments — are off-label and follow an individual evaluation.

Most recently I was an assistant professor at UCSF, where I built the general physiatry service, grew pelvic floor and hypermobility care, and did diagnostic EMG. Before that I was associate medical director of the acute rehab unit at Dominican Hospital in Santa Cruz.

Duke for MD and undergrad (and varsity fencing). Internship at UCSF Fresno. Residency at Shirley Ryan AbilityLab in Chicago — including performing arts medicine coverage at Ravinia. I love mentoring the next generation of physicians and therapists.

I grew up in South Texas and Spain. I speak English, Spanish, Turkish, and German; we’ll confirm language fit for your visits.

Outside clinic: spouse, two kids, time outdoors together, sourdough, and a mediocre clarinet habit that keeps me humble.

This is a new solo practice. It is not a hospital clinic.
